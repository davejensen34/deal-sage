# Reviewer workflow audit

September 15, 2026 · Issue [#154](https://github.com/davejensen34/deal-sage/issues/154) · Baseline `9ec2522` (Milestone 7 closeout).

## Product assessment

DealSage has a substantial evidence and research foundation, but its everyday reviewer workflow is incomplete. A developer can assemble and evaluate research that a reviewer cannot yet initiate, investigate and develop through the app. The main product gap is this disconnected workflow, not the absence of another model or a new confidence score.

The user's intended outcome is **reviewer-driven insight into recent business transitions that may merit relevant marketing communications or further opportunity development**. Acquisition after an owner's death is one possible path, not a conclusion generated by the event itself. A leadership change can support a business-introduction hypothesis without proving ownership. An owner death can support a continuity question without proving a sale, distress or a successor.

The recommended journey is **Discover → Review → Develop → Monitor**. Keep one durable research case as the underlying record, support signal-first/business-first/hybrid starts, and make the reviewer decision a separate layer from source assertions and DealSage interpretation.

## Inspection and limits

Inspected all routed surfaces: Overview, Candidates, Candidate Detail, Research (including source operations), Watchlists, Evidence, Activity, Settings, Login and Evaluation Review. Read their components plus API routes, models, research loop/planner, source refresh, confidence, business briefs and conclusion services; checked ADR-002/003/004/007/008 context and Milestone 7's actual outcomes. GitHub had only deferred #92 open before this planning issue.

The live service on localhost:3000 was stopped. Rendered inspection used current frontend code on port 5186 and a newly migrated, credential-free SQLite demo API on loopback port 8000. It seeded 18 fictional candidates and no research cases. Providers were disabled, `.env` was not used, and the actual research database/corpus was untouched. This audit therefore does not claim fresh Google authentication, a production deployment, live discovery or exhaustive testing of every mutation/role. A missing-candidate browser probe remained in its loading state during the bounded observation; the missing error handling below is also visible in source. An empty-database dashboard probe independently reproduced `ZeroDivisionError` in an in-memory store.

The [interactive proposal](design/milestone-8/index.html) uses four fictional examples. It illustrates a retirement handoff, a possible owner death, a management-only change and an unresolved person/business clue. Its state is page-local and is not actual analyst feedback.

At 390px the candidate page uses internal horizontal scrolling and icon-only navigation rather than overflowing the viewport. This is functional but makes comparison and action discovery difficult. The proposed design uses stacked lead cards with labeled navigation and a focused brief.

## The record-ceiling question

There is no single global 50-record ceiling:

- The old Colorado experiment fetches 50 and reports that fixed historical cohort (`research/experiment.py`).
- The current refresh UI sends 25; its API/service validate 1–100 (`SourceRefreshes.tsx`, `domain/schemas.py`, `research/refresh.py`).
- Case narratives expose at most 20 cases without paging (`api/routes.py`).
- Candidate pages and exports have separate 100-record ceilings; the UI shows ten candidates per page.
- Search-result limits are environment-managed, while planner limits already exist per case.

Milestone 8 should make **research scope configurable in the database**, within hard safety/provider/source caps. Page size, unique discovered records, retained documents, model calls, elapsed time and spend are separate controls with separate units. Count rejected/duplicate candidates and failures explicitly; do not imply a request for 100 records promises 100 business matches. Utah delivery size is an import property, not an exemption from downstream research budgets.

## Product decisions recommended for approval

1. Put reviewer triage first. Navigation: Review inbox, Discover, Opportunity briefs, Monitor, Sources & runs; evidence/history remain reachable from the brief. Keep operator settings separate.
2. Use **research lead → reviewer-selected development brief → explicit outreach decision**. A development brief can be exploratory and ownership-uncertain. Existing ownership/promotion guards remain unchanged; no automatic candidate promotion is needed to monitor an unresolved lead.
3. Evaluate commercial fit separately from evidence confidence. Management-only changes may support a relevant introduction but not an ownership-transition claim. A mortality-led acquisition hypothesis needs stronger person/business linkage and explicit review of current business contact and timing.
4. Preserve source assertions, original model interpretation and reviewer corrections visibly. Lead with what changed and why it may matter; put detailed scoring/provenance on demand without hiding material contradictions.
5. Make one useful, bounded workflow work end to end before adding a CRM, email delivery, scheduled crawling or more charts.

These are proposed product decisions, not implemented policy changes. Milestone 8 remains proposed until the user approves its concrete plan and design.
